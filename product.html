The science behind it

The American College of Sports Medicine recommends that people who sweat heavily replace sodium along with fluid, because sweat carries significant amounts of sodium and drinking plain water alone does not replace it. Sodium in a drink also helps stimulate thirst and supports fluid retention after exercise.

Sawka, M. N., Burke, L. M., Eichner, E. R., Maughan, R. J., Montain, S. J., & Stachenfeld, N. S. (2007). American College of Sports Medicine position stand: Exercise and fluid replacement. Medicine & Science in Sports & Exercise, 39(2), 377–390.

How do I take it?

Tear one stick into 400–600ml of cold water and shake. Take one in the morning, around training, or whenever you've been sweating. Night sticks go in warm or cold water about 30 minutes before bed.

Isn't that a lot of sodium?

It's designed for people who sweat: athletes, outdoor workers, hot climates, long flights. If you're on a low-sodium diet or have a medical condition, check with your doctor before using it.
